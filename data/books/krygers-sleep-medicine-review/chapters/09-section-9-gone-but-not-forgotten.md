# Section 9 Gone but Not Forgotten

<!-- pdf-page: 948 -->

### SECTION 9

The biggest surprise for applicants taking previous sleep medicine board examinations was the discrepancy between the expected and actual number of pediatrics questions. According to previous exam blueprints, only 2% of the exam was to cover “considerations and disorders unique to childhood.” Thus many test takers did not study pediatrics at all, assuming it worthwhile to sacrifice 2% of the exam for the benefit of not having to study a large area of sleep medicine. They might also have assumed that a board that deals with adult medicine (the American Board of Internal Medicine [ABIM]) would not have a great deal of pediatrics material in its exam. Unfortunately, they were wrong; past exams have contained many pediatric questions.

## Why This Chapter?

The current blueprint omits any specific mention of unique pediatric syndromes. It is still very likely that pediatric cases will be interspersed in various sections of the exam; thus the pediatric aspect of the exam appears to be a work in progress.

Surprisingly, pediatrics cases that appeared in other sections of the exam often dealt with esoteric conditions seldom seen in the sleep laboratory; for example, in 2009, several questions covered sleep apnea in children with achondroplasia. The message here is that you cannot safely omit a review of pediatric sleep medicine. Thus you will find pediatric cases dispersed throughout this book. In this section we review some areas unique to children.
